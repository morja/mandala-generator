# ADR-0010 · Export formats and framing: portrait images with the mandala placed by hand

Status: accepted, 20 October 2026 (Mathis' decisions; points 1 to 3 first, the rest as recommended in [PLAN-2](../PLAN-2-portrait-export.md); framing as a Pro feature, strips and 2048 wide portrait decided after [SPIKE-1](../SPIKE-1-banded-rendering.md); centred backgrounds follow the mandala, decided after trying it).

## Decision

1. **Portrait is free.** Every format can be saved at the free size, with the mandala centred. Pro (ADR-0007) adds higher resolution and framing by hand (point 4), not formats.
2. **Android only.** The macOS app does not get formats or framing. This is an exception to "Mac first" (`AGENTS.md`, ADR-0008): it is an export option of the phone app, not a motif.
3. **The format is chosen when saving.** It is not part of the mandala: not in the settings JSON, not in favourites or history (ADR-0003 is untouched). The app remembers the last format and framing as a convenience. *Since 26 October 2026 (Mathis):* each format keeps its own placement (Whole, Fill or by hand), starting on Whole, so a Fill or a hand placement in one format no longer carries into another; the square, which has no Whole/Fill switch, shows "Reset" once placed by hand.
4. **Framing by hand, a Pro feature.** After picking a format the user places the mandala in the frame: drag to move, pinch to zoom, double tap to reset. Zoomed in, only a section of the mandala is saved; zoomed out, it sits anywhere on the background. Without Pro the default framing is used (mandala centred, fitting the shorter side); the framing controls are visible with a Pro mark and open the Pro sheet, like "Save large" today.
   *Since 22 October 2026 (Mathis):* two framings are free in every format but the square: **Show whole** (as before) and **Fill**, the mandala zoomed until it fills the frame to the corners (`MandalaRenderer.fillZoom`, measured on the mandala's own layers). With Pro they also end placing by hand. No third, deeper fill: Pro users zoom by hand.
5. **The canvas is extended, never cropped from a square.** The renderer draws a canvas of any width and height with the mandala at a given centre and size. Zooming in renders real detail at the output resolution, not enlarged pixels.
6. **What moves with the mandala, what stays with the canvas.** The mandala layers (and the emblem) move and scale with the framing, including everything that depends on their size: line widths, glow, wash, painted effect, brightness scale. Background and the effects layer (grain, stars, glitter, vignette, bloom, local contrast, relief) belong to the canvas, with one exception: backgrounds with a centre (auto, radial gradient, sunburst) sit at the mandala's centre, so they stay behind it when it moves; the auto background, which is drawn from the first layer around the mandala, also takes its size. Linear gradient, grain and image stay on the canvas.
7. **Text layer: a choice in the framing step.** "Text stays in place" (default): the quote is placed in canvas coordinates, so it can sit in the free space below the mandala. "Text moves with the mandala": it moves and scales with the framing. Like the format, the choice belongs to the saved image, not to the settings.
8. **Zoom range** (Pro) from a quarter to four times "fits the width"; the mandala's centre always stays inside the frame.
9. **Sizes.** Free: 1024 px on the shorter side in every format, drawn at twice the size and scaled down, as today. Pro: 2048 px on the shorter side, without supersampling, so it is the free export's internal image, as today for the square (ADR-0007): 2048 × 2048, 2048 × 2560 for 4:5, 3641 × 2048 for 16:9, about 2048 × 4437 for a 9:19.5 phone screen.
10. **"Set as wallpaper"** in the framing step, through Android's `WallpaperManager` (home screen, lock screen or both). A wallpaper fills the screen, so it is always set in the "This screen" format (the sheet switches to it first, so the frame shows what is set), 2048 px wide with Pro, 1024 without.
    *Since 22 October 2026 (Mathis):* "Set as wallpaper" from the menu always uses **Fill**; from the save sheet it uses what the frame shows.
11. **Square with the default framing is today's image.** Format square, mandala centred at the width of the canvas, must render pixel-identical to today, so `ParityTest` and its thresholds stay valid.
12. **Every step that works on a full-size image does it in strips** and keeps only the 8-bit result ([SPIKE-1](../SPIKE-1-banded-rendering.md)): layer lines, Strange Attractor points, graffiti, drawing layer, effects layer, stars and glitter; glow and wash write into the layer image instead of a copy. The image stays byte-identical and the render time the same; the one exception is the graffiti blur, whose running sums start at the strip and may differ by one step of 255 in single pixels. This is what makes 2048 × 4437 fit into 256 MB (ADR-0002); without it neither the free nor the Pro portrait export fits.

Formats offered: **Square**, **This screen** (aspect ratio of the device's screen, taller side over shorter, at most 21:9; the format for wallpapers) and, in a menu "Other ratio", portrait **4:5, 3:4, 2:3, 9:16** and landscape **5:4, 4:3, 3:2, 16:9** (added 22 October 2026 at Mathis' request). The mandala fits the canvas's shorter side, so square and portrait are unchanged and a landscape canvas extends to the sides. More can follow without a new decision.

10. **Moving picture** (26 October 2026, Mathis, UX-1 12, DM-16 A, Pro per ADR-0016). The save sheet switches between "Picture" and "Moving"; the moving picture keeps format, framing, finish and the text choice. The layers turn against each other as in the macOS animation export (first, third and fifth one way, the others the other way), each one whole turn per loop, so the loop has no jump; background, emblem and text stand still. Lengths 5, 10 or 15 s at 24 frames a second; 720 or 1080 px on the shorter side, at most 1920 on the longer one (players and the apps videos are shared to, and the memory budget with five kept layers), drawn without supersampling like the Pro picture. File: H.264 in MP4, BT.709 limited range, written with Android's own `MediaCodec` and `MediaMuxer` (no package); the encoder and the file live in the app, the colour conversion in `core` (`VideoFrames`). This is the one file format the app writes itself, an exception to `android/AGENTS.md` approved with the decision. GIF and the macOS app's MOV are not offered for now.

## How it is built

- `RenderOptions` has a canvas (`width`, `height`), a `Framing` (`centreX`, `centreY` as fractions of the canvas, `zoom` relative to "fits the shorter side", clamped to 0.25–4 with the centre inside the frame) and `textFollowsMandala`. `outputSize` stays as the square shortcut.
- The mandala layers, hand layers and emblem are drawn for a square of side `shorter canvas side × zoom` around the centre (`Placement`); background and effects layer use the canvas. Each mandala layer keeps the buffer square the macOS port uses (its size and effect margin as before), placed around the centre, and only the part that can reach the canvas is drawn: the canvas plus the effect margin for an unrotated layer, the whole square for a rotated one, or, when that square is more than twice the canvas, the part within reach of the canvas corners. Lines far outside are rejected in the coordinates of that whole square, so zooming in loses none. Layers are blended at their offset; outside a layer counts as black, which only matters for multiply.
- Layer buffers on a canvas larger than the mandala (22 October 2026, after Mathis saw a hard edge above and below the mandala on his lock screen): many styles draw past the macOS buffer of twice the radius (Star Burst to 1.05 radii, Hyperboloid 1.4, Tendril and Tesseract 2.1, Deep Nebula fills what it gets). The Mac never shows that area, a portrait, landscape or zoomed-out canvas does, so the buffer edge showed as a straight line. When the canvas reaches past the centred square and its margin, or the layer is moved, the layer is drawn in a square as large as its style reaches (`MandalaRenderer.styleReach`, measured per style plus about 5 %, capped at 2.5 radii for memory) plus the effect margin; `StyleReachTest` fails when a style draws farther. The centred square keeps the old buffers and stays byte-identical (`ParityTest` unchanged); the middle of a portrait now differs from the square image by about 68 dB, because its glow is blurred on the larger buffer. A free portrait takes about 25 % longer.
- Line width (both apps, 22 October 2026): lines are hairlines up to a buffer of 1024 px, the Android preview's. Drawn larger (an export, a larger Mac preview, or zoomed in), a layer gets proportionally wider lines, spread as ceil(width) hairlines sharing the weight, and keeps the brightness scale of 1024 px, so an export looks like the preview enlarged instead of thinner and darker. Universe and Symbols, which already grow their elements with the size, keep hairlines. Images up to 1024 px buffer are unchanged; the Mac's own look gets slightly bolder (its preview is drawn at 1600 px). First found on the phone for zoomed images (21 October 2026), widened to every size after Mathis noticed exports losing brightness on both apps.
- Zoomed in, the effect margin grows with the mandala; an unrotated layer is therefore cut to what the painted effect still reads before painting, and the painted effect works in place.
- Reference blurs (glow, bloom) on a tall canvas are computed in strips of rows once the reference image is larger than a 1700 px square, so a portrait canvas never holds several full reference copies.
- Backgrounds take width and height. Radial ones (radial gradient, sunburst, auto) stay round and sit at the mandala's centre; radial gradient and sunburst measure their radius on the canvas's shorter side, the auto background on the mandala's square; a linear gradient runs edge to edge; an image is stretched to the square of the longer side and its middle is used. In the effects layer, spots, highlights, stars and glitter spread over the whole canvas and their number grows with its area.
- The text layer is laid out in a frame: the canvas (font size from the shorter side, vertical position from the height) or the mandala's square. The platform text renderer draws masks of width × height.
- Memory: the budget rule (ADR-0002, `MemoryBudgetTest`) holds for the Pro portrait 2048 × 4437 zoomed in to 4 and the free portrait 1024 × 2219, and for the Pro landscape 3641 × 2048 zoomed in to 4.

- **Moving picture:** each layer is drawn once as a turning layer and kept with its image before turning (`LayerCache`, `RenderOptions.turningLayers`), cut to what can turn onto the canvas; what stands still (background, drawing layer, emblem, text masks, stars and glitter) is kept too. A frame then only turns and blends the layers and runs the effects chain, so it equals a render of that frame (the kept glows of emblem and text are 16-bit fixed point, at most one step of 255 off, `LayerAnimationTest`).

## Reasons

- A section cut from the square loses resolution (1152 × 2048 out of 2048 × 2048 for 9:16) and cuts the mandala at the sides. That is the opposite of what a wallpaper needs.
- Placing by hand covers both wishes with one gesture: a detail of the mandala filling the screen, or the whole mandala low on the lock screen below the clock, with the background continuing.
- Portrait for free: wallpapers are the main use on a phone, and shared wallpapers advertise the app (same reasoning as ADR-0007). Framing by hand and the sharper image are a stronger reason to buy Pro than resolution alone.
- Keeping the format out of the settings JSON keeps compatibility with the macOS app simple: a favourite looks the same on both, and the format is a decision about one saved image.

## Later

- Moving and zooming each layer on its own (Mathis' idea, 20 October 2026): new looks, for example the background zoomed towards one corner and a layer towards another. That would be part of the mandala, so it needs settings fields and belongs to the Mac first (ADR-0008).

- 4096 square: not needed for now (Mathis, 20 October 2026). It would need strips through the whole pipeline, about 2 to 3 times the render time (SPIKE-1).
